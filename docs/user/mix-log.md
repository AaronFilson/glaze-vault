# The mix log

The mix log keeps each batch as it was mixed: the day, how much, the lot or bag of each material
weighed and, for a slip, the water and deflocculant that went in and how it read as it aged. It
is encrypted like your recipes.

## Logging a mix

Open a recipe and choose **Log a mix…** below it, or open a batch sheet and choose **Log a mix…**
there. You can also open **Mix log** at the top, choose **Log a mix**, then the batch sheet or
recipe under **Mixed from**. A mix belongs to the revision of the sheet or recipe that was mixed.

Fill in what you know; only what was mixed and the day are needed:

- **Mixed on** and **Dry batch size,** in grams, kilograms, pounds or ounces. With a size, the
  table shows each material's share and weight, as the batch sheet does.
- **Lot:** the lot or bag of each material, clays most of all. A new bag of a clay can change a
  result, and this is how you trace it.
- **Notes,** then **Save**.

What you type is kept, encrypted, if the vault locks before you save, as for firings.

**Mixes (2)** below a recipe or a batch sheet shows its mixes. In the mix log, **Search** finds
mixes by name, day, lots and notes.

## A slip as it is mixed and aged

**Water, deflocculant and readings** opens by itself for a slip. It shows the recipe's targets
for specific gravity and Ford cup seconds, if it has them.

1. **Water at the start:** what you put in first, in the batch's unit.
2. **Add a step** for each addition and each reading. A step has its day, the **Deflocculant
   added** and **Water added**, in the batch's unit, and what the slip read after: its
   **Specific gravity**, its seconds through a **Ford cup #4**, and how it **gels** standing.
3. Come back as the slip ages, open the mix and add a step for each day's reading. The day of
   each step after the first shows how many days the slip had aged.

Below the steps, **Deflocculant in all** and **Water in all** add up what went in, with the
share of the dry batch: 37.5 g of Darvan in a 5 kg batch is 0.75%.

A common way to tune a casting slip is to start with about 95% of the recipe's water and 75% of
its deflocculant, mix long, then add deflocculant a little at a time until it flows, and water
only if the gravity is too high. See
[clay body formulation](../research/clay-body-formulation.md#8-casting-slips) for more.

## Lots on a firing

When you log a firing of the same recipe revision, **Material lots** offers **Take the lots from
a mix**: choose the mix, by its day, and its lots fill in. See [the firing log](firing-log.md).

## Deleting a mix

**Delete…** removes the mix for good. Backups made before then still have it.

## Who sees it

In a [shared vault](sharing.md), a mix is in the collection of what was mixed. A batch operator
reads and logs mixes of their batch sheets, and sees the sheet's codes rather than the
materials. Testers, formulators and admins log mixes too; chemists read them. Someone who reads
without chemistry does not see them.

A batch operator also sees mixes someone made from a recipe without a batch sheet: their lots
and readings, but not which recipe it is.
